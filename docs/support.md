# Support this work

Core-Geth is one of the public goods maintained in the [`ethereumclassic`](https://github.com/ethereumclassic)
organization, alongside the network's bootnodes and the
[peer discovery lists](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/discv4-dns-lists) that nodes start from. This page says
who maintains and funds them, why they need sponsors, and how a sponsor is credited. It is the one place this
project publishes its funding routes. If you find them anywhere else, check them against this page first.

## Who maintains them

The [Ethereum Classic DAO](https://ethereumclassicdao.org), a Wyoming DAO LLC, is the legal entity that maintains
these public goods, together with Ethereum Classic's core developers and community contributors. The work is done
in the `ethereumclassic` organization, where each repository has more than one admin and more than one maintainer.
The repositories stay there no matter who maintains or funds them.

That arrangement is new. Until now, this work moved between organizations as each one closed or left: ETCDEV in
2018, ETC Labs in 2021, and the ETC Cooperative, which entered maintenance mode at the end of 2024 and is due to
dissolve. Along the way, the client changed repositories, the bootnodes and discovery trees
changed operators, and Core-Geth went 21 months without security maintenance. A standing legal entity, with the
software kept in the community's own organization, is meant to end that churn.

With the ETC Cooperative's dissolution, Ethereum Classic stakeholders such as mining pools, exchanges and service
providers should use <security@ethereumclassic.net> as their point of contact. A person answers it: one of the
core developers who maintain this work. Sponsorship has its own address, given below.

## Who has funded this work

These public goods have been funded by the following organizations. [Project history](about/project-history.md)
tells how the work passed between them.

| Years | Organization |
|---|---|
| 2016 to 2018 | [ETCDEV](https://web.archive.org/web/20190330063218/https://www.etcdevteam.com/) |
| 2019 to 2021 | [ETC Labs](https://web.archive.org/web/20200425081322/https://etclabs.org/) |
| 2017 to 2026 | [ETC Cooperative](https://web.archive.org/web/20250205192722/https://etccooperative.org/) |
| 2025 to present | [Ethereum Classic DAO](https://ethereumclassicdao.org) |

The Ethereum Classic DAO pays for this work today, as its funder of last resort, on top of maintaining it. It was
[launched in May 2025](https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/business/FilingDetails.aspx?eFNum=101157225123250204055197221085227098244002050082) and took up Core-Geth's maintenance in February 2026. By then the client had gone without a
release since June 2024, and the ETC Cooperative had been in maintenance mode since the end of 2024. The DAO pays
for the client's development, the bootnode servers, and the domains these services are published under.

**Sponsors are credited here.** A sponsoring organization is added to this table with its name, a link and the
years it has contributed, and can be credited for the specific work it funds. A sponsor that prefers not to be
named is not listed.

## Why sponsor

If you are a stakeholder of Ethereum Classic, you rely on this infrastructure. Mining pools, centralized exchanges
and custodians, issuers of Ethereum Classic financial products, mining hardware manufacturers, block explorers and
RPC providers all depend on a client that follows the network and on peer discovery that works every night.
**Ethereum Classic has no protocol-layer funding mechanism for its critical infrastructure or its core
developers**, so no part of the network's fee or issuance revenue pays for this work.

The organization that maintains it is in place. What it needs is steady funding from the stakeholders who depend
on it, so that the work no longer rests on a single backer.

## How to sponsor

**An invoiced agreement, for organizations.** If your business depends on this client, a maintenance sponsorship
can be invoiced against a purchase order like any other vendor relationship, which is usually what a finance
department needs. Write to <donations@ethereumclassic.net> with what you run and the period you want to cover.

**A direct transfer.** For anyone who prefers to send funds directly, an address is provided on request. Write to
<donations@ethereumclassic.net>. If a large transfer is planned, send a small one first and confirm receipt at
the same address.

**This project does not publish a donation address.** An address shown in a screenshot, a fork, a mirror or a
message did not come from this project unless it arrived in a reply from <donations@ethereumclassic.net>.

Funding mail goes to `ethereumclassic.net`, the domain for the public goods maintained in the `ethereumclassic`
organization.

## What sponsorship pays for

Keeping a consensus client fit to run a live network: tracking upstream security fixes and deciding which apply
to Ethereum Classic, cutting and verifying releases across nine platforms, running the bootnodes, and measuring
what the published binaries actually contain. The [audits](audits/2026-03-security-audit.md) are the record of
that work, including what was found broken and what it took to fix.
[The discovery lists' support page](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/discv4-dns-lists/blob/main/docs/support.md)
describes what it pays for in the trees.

## What sponsorship does not buy

**No influence over consensus rules, release content, or what an audit reports.** Chain configuration follows
the ECIP process, and every finding this project publishes is measured and linked so it can be checked without
trusting either the maintainers or anyone who funds them. A sponsor gets the same client as everyone else, on
the same schedule. Being credited as a sponsor records a contribution. It is not an endorsement of the sponsor's
products or services.

Requests for a feature, a fix, or a supported configuration are welcome from anyone, sponsor or not, in
[the repository's issues](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/core-geth/issues).
